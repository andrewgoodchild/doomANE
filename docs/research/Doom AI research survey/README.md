# Model the map's machinery, then dodge

Ten years of Doom AI work give the bot two clear next steps. **For route knowledge, parse the WAD's linedef and sector specials into a state-space progression planner.** Use live ViZDoom sector heights to confirm that lifts, doors and switches actually moved. **For combat survival, add the reflex layer that every successful Doom bot has:** missile avoidance, strafing while firing, engage distances by weapon, and splash-safe firing. Laya has to authorize these through a combat-movement option, or they must run inside the `attack` command's execution. Nobody has solved either problem for us. No published agent of any kind (RL, imitation, LLM or scripted) reports completion rates on held-out campaign maps. The only scripted bot with a real progression planner, arthurjdam/doom-mcp, completes just two shareware maps, and its gaps are the same as ours: lifts, some trigger types and boss exits. So our MAP01-03 numbers and the 0/18 held-out score are frontier data, not a regression against prior art. Learned approaches stay a longer-term bet. Sample Factory is the best open combat policy, but it was trained on deathmatch, its transfer to Freedoom monsters is unproven, and it would break the rule that the model picks target and weapon. World models are a lossy copy of an engine we already have. The ranked lists below include effort estimates, conflicts with the project rules and the strength of the evidence.

## Nobody has published held-out campaign results, so our 0/18 is new data

The literature splits into four camps, and none of them reaches where we are. **Pixel LLM agents clear nothing.** VideoGameBench scored 0% on Doom II for Gemini 2.5 Pro, GPT-4o, Claude 3.7 Sonnet and others, in both the real-time and paused modes ([vgbench.com](https://www.vgbench.com/)). De Wynter's GPT-4V agent completed 0 of 10 runs of E1M1 even with a hand-written walkthrough, and died in 40-90% of them ([arXiv 2403.05468](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.05468v1)). **Structured LLM agents clear one map at a time.** lucas-moont/doom-player went from 1/5 to 4/5 on E1M1 when it added the automap, and to 5/5 with a notebook, at about 12.6M tokens and 11.7 minutes per attempt ([PR #28](https://github.com/lucas-moont/doom-player/pull/28)). doomLaya v0.3.1 cleared MAP01-03 once each with zero deaths ([doomLaya README](https://github.com/azalio/doomLaya)). **RL agents do not learn keys.** PPO cleared E1M1 15/15 with exit-distance shaping, but the E1M1 policies scored 0/5 on E1M2 and never picked up the red key ([PR #31](https://github.com/lucas-moont/doom-player/pull/31), [PR #32](https://github.com/lucas-moont/doom-player/pull/32)). The only RL key result is 80% on E1M2 from two 10M-step runs with key-shaped reward on that one map ([doom-player](https://github.com/lucas-moont/doom-player)). GameNGen's 50M-step PPO agent "still does not explore all of the game's locations" ([GameNGen](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.14837)). **Scripted bots are the closest peers.** arthurjdam/doom-mcp plans on a 16-unit grid, chains key and switch prerequisites, and completes E1M1 and E1M2 ([doom-mcp](https://github.com/arthurjdam/doom-mcp)).

Combat was never solved in this field either. The 2016-2017 ViZDoom competition organisers found that no bot dodged missiles, that strafing was rare, and that splash weapons fired at nearby walls caused many suicides. One author "can consistently defeat all of them" ([Wydmuch et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1809.03470)). The winners did best by combining a learned core with hard-coded reflexes. Arnold4 was hard-coded to run straight after about 1 s of standing still, and YanShi took second place on unknown maps with a detector plus SLAM, MCTS and hand-written dodging and corner-escape rules ([Wydmuch et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1809.03470)). That is the same architecture class as ours, and it supports extending the executor instead of replacing it.

## Route knowledge: four cheap pieces turn WAD specials into plans

The held-out failure is mechanisms the navigator does not model, and the evidence points strongly one way. Lifts, switches and triggers can be derived statically from the WAD, so learning them is unnecessary. ViZDoom exposes live `floor_height` and `ceiling_height` for every sector, rebuilt on every state update. It exposes no line special, tag or sector special ([viz_game.cpp](https://github.com/Farama-Foundation/ViZDoom/blob/master/src/vizdoom/src/viz_game.cpp), [game_state.md](https://github.com/Farama-Foundation/ViZDoom/blob/master/docs/api/python/game_state.md)). Specials and tags must therefore come from the WAD, for example through omgifol (MIT) ([omgifol](https://github.com/devinacker/omgifol)), and be joined to ViZDoom sectors by index. The sector join is clean, because ViZDoom's sector `id` is the engine index. **The line join is unverified:** ViZDoom builds lines through a `lineIds` map, so check its order against the WAD's endpoints on MAP04 before relying on it.

Otherwise waiting and stepping on count as unauthorized movement. |
| R6 | **ViZDoom 1.3 perception inputs.** Read the notifications buffer (for example "You need a blue key") and `state.objects` categories and `sector_id`. | Low | Added in 1.3.0 and 1.3.1, and the arm64 wheels exist ([ViZDoom releases](https://github.com/Farama-Foundation/ViZDoom/releases)). | No conflict. | R7 | **Event-triggered System 2 over the objective graph.** Call Qwen on objective failure, being stuck, entering a new region or death. R1 and R2 come first because they attack the held-out limit directly, and almost all of their work is parsing and bookkeeping. R3 and R4 decide whether the parsed mechanisms turn into the right order of objectives. R4 is the only design that can be ported outright under our licence rules. For MAP04 specifically, no speedrun route, TAS or walkthrough exists for Freedoom's "Containment Bay" ([CREDITS-LEVELS](https://github.com/freedoom/freedoom/blob/master/CREDITS-LEVELS)). A `debug_cut` printout from our own planner is the only reliable way to check the blue-key route.

## Combat survival: the Cajun bot's reflexes, authorized by the model

The MAP04 diagnosis is 15-50 HP after the red key and 5 deaths in 8 blue-key attempts. That is a reflex problem at 35 Hz, not a choice problem at 2 Hz. The GZDoom Cajun bot's combat loop is about 100 lines under a 3-clause BSD-style licence, so it can be ported with attribution ([b_think.cpp](https://github.com/ZDoom/gzdoom/blob/master/src/playsim/bots/b_think.cpp)). doom-mcp's pilot is a second, GPL design reference ([combat.rs](https://github.com/arthurjdam/doom-mcp/blob/HEAD/src/pilot/combat.rs)). The constraint is authority. Clean v0.3.1 failed with "Unauthorized left strafe", so every movement reflex must sit inside a command Laya chose, or be a movement option Laya picks. doomLaya already has a published version of the second path: its `factorized` mode asks a separate "combat movement" question ([doomLaya README](https://github.com/azalio/doomLaya)).

| Rank | Suggestion | Effort | Evidence | Rule notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | **Missile avoidance.** When a tracked projectile is within about 686 map units, face it, backpedal and strafe at full speed. Flip the strafe direction (5-tic cooldown) if position barely changes. Keep firing if the enemy is within a 60° cone. | Low | Cajun's top-priority rule ([b_think.cpp](https://github.com/ZDoom/gzdoom/blob/master/src/playsim/bots/b_think.cpp)). ViZDoom lists projectiles in `state.objects`, so no engine change is needed. No competition bot dodged ([Wydmuch et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1809.03470)). | Run it inside `attack` execution, or expose `evade` as a model-chosen movement option. It must not trigger during `explore` or `pickup` unless the model chose it. |
| C2 | **Strafing and engage distance by weapon while attacking.** Strafe continuously and flip every about 30 tics or when stuck. Close in if the enemy is beyond the weapon's range and back off if nearer. Back away from melee monsters (Demon, Spectre, Lost Soul) inside 160 units. No ledge drops with melee weapons. | Low | doom-mcp's pilot clears all 6 monsters on E1M1 in about 26 s of game time ([doom-mcp README](https://github.com/arthurjdam/doom-mcp)). Arnold2/4 added strafing in 2017 ([Wydmuch et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1809.03470)). | Same authority path as C1. Distances depend on the weapon Laya chose and do not change that choice. |
| C3 | **Firing hygiene.** Don't fire rockets within 256 units, or when a trace projectile shows impact closer than 140 units. Fire only when the crosshair is within the target's angular size (at least 2.5°). Lead projectile shots by about velocity × distance / speed. | Low | Cajun's `Dofire`/`FireRox` ([b_func.cpp](https://github.com/ZDoom/gzdoom/blob/master/src/playsim/bots/b_func.cpp)) and doom-mcp ([combat.rs](https://github.com/arthurjdam/doom-mcp/blob/HEAD/src/pilot/combat.rs)). Splash at nearby walls was a main source of suicides in 2017 ([Wydmuch et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1809.03470)). | Allowed: this is how a chosen weapon fires, not which weapon. **Conflict:** doom-mcp's executor-side weapon choice by range and its target priority must not be ported, because the model chooses both. |
| C4 | **Give Laya the survival information.** Put HP, armour, visible projectiles and health items in its state, and add `retreat` (break line of sight along the navigator path) and "pickup health mid-fight" as options. | Low-medium | Cajun grabs a medikit mid-fight when health is below a threshold ([b_think.cpp](https://github.com/ZDoom/gzdoom/blob/master/src/playsim/bots/b_think.cpp)). Marvin, the 2017 Track 1 winner, picked up the most medkits (354) ([Wydmuch et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1809.03470)). | **Conflict if the executor does it.** Cajun's health grab sets item usefulness. Here it must be a model-chosen option, and Laya likely needs retraining on examples that use it. |
| C5 | **DFP-style survival head.** Laya predicts Δhealth and Δprogress over the next few seconds for each candidate command, then scores the candidates with runtime weights ("survive" vs "push"). | Medium | IntelAct (DFP) won the 2016 unknown-map track with a 3.08 frags-per-death ratio, and its goal weights can change at test time without retraining ([Wydmuch et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1809.03470), [arXiv 1611.01779](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.01779)). Arnold's auxiliary "enemy visible" head from engine labels lifted its kills-per-death ratio from under 2 to over 4 at 65 h ([arXiv 1609.05521](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1609.05521)). | Fits the rules: the model still decides. Train only on our own logs, never on hosted outputs. Untested on typed commands. |
| C6 | **A short reaction gate.** Don't start the first shot or a weapon switch inside about a 35-tic switch cooldown, and pause firing while the screen is still red from a heavy hit. | Low | Cajun ([b_func.cpp](https://github.com/ZDoom/gzdoom/blob/master/src/playsim/bots/b_func.cpp)) and doom-mcp ([combat.rs](https://github.com/arthurjdam/doom-mcp/blob/HEAD/src/pilot/combat.rs)). | Weakest evidence of the list. Cajun's reaction delay exists to make it beatable, not to keep it alive, so keep only the switch cooldown. |

C1-C3 are a few days of executor work, backed by the longest-lived bot code in Doom. **What is unproven is how much they help us.** None of the surveyed sources (VideoGameBench, de Wynter, doom-mcp, doomLaya or the competition papers) reports deaths per minute or damage taken for any bot, so the gain must be measured on MAP02-04 dev runs (deaths per 20 minutes and HP after the red key) before anyone claims it. C4 matters most where it meets the project rules: survival depends on the model knowing it is hurt and having somewhere to go.

## Longer-term bets: learned combat heads and generated maps

**A 1-10M-parameter combat head on the ANE at 10-35 Hz** is the most compute-feasible learned upgrade. SauerkrautLM-Doom-MultiVec has 1.3M parameters and was trained on about 2 hours of one human playing. It scored 178 frags against 13 for all tested LLMs combined on defend_the_center, at 31 ms per decision on CPU. But it reached only 57.7% validation accuracy and was tested on one scenario ([arXiv 2604.07385](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.07385v1)). It would need DAgger-style correction, and it would have to output movement and aim for the target and weapon Laya chose. **Sample Factory** (MIT, maintained) is the strongest open combat policy. It beats the hardest built-in bots in 100% of matches, and checkpoints are on HuggingFace ([arXiv 2006.11751](https://arxiv.org/html/2006.11751v2), [SF docs](https://www.samplefactory.dev/09-environment-integrations/vizdoom/)). Treat it as a teacher to distil from, not a drop-in. It picks its own targets and weapons (a rule conflict), it trained on deathmatch with about 2.5B frames per agent, it has no Freedoom single-player results, and the checkpoints' licences are unchecked. **Oblige/Obsidian-generated maps** give an unlimited dev set with keys, switches and lifts, and keep MAP05-15 untouched. With affordance-augmented A*, navigation on 15 unseen Oblige maps improved by about 45% over a geometry-only planner ([arXiv 2001.02364](https://arxiv.org/html/2001.02364v2)). Oblige's quest tree is a GPL design reference for objective graphs ([quest.lua](https://github.com/obsidian-level-maker/Obsidian/blob/master/scripts/quest.lua)). **Route imitation from `.lmp` demos** has no published precedent, and no MAP04 demo was found. **Reachability networks** (SPTM, episodic curiosity, Apache-2.0 code) could detect being stuck or a newly opened region ([arXiv 1810.02274](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.02274v5)), but none of these agents fights. **World models rank lowest.** GameNGen remembers about 3 s, needed 128 TPU-v5e and released no code ([GameNGen](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.14837v2)). Dream-trained Take Cover controllers learned to exploit a model in which monsters stopped shooting ([worldmodels.github.io](https://worldmodels.github.io/)). Rehearse fights in the real engine instead.

## Conclusion

The literature changes the question we should ask. Its successful agents did not learn the hard parts. They encoded the map's mechanics (doom-mcp, crustygen) and hard-coded the reflexes (Cajun, Arnold4, YanShi), and used learning or language only for choices. That is our architecture already, so the gaps are engineering debts, not research problems. The authority rule is the one real design constraint: every reflex has to be expressible as part of a command Laya chose. The cheapest compliant path is doomLaya's own factorized combat-movement question, extended with `evade` and `retreat`.

Two cautions follow. First, the held-out rule makes R8's cross-death memory and any MAP04-specific tuning easy to contaminate, so build and measure on MAP01-04 and generated maps, and run MAP05-15 only as a frozen check. Second, because no source reports deaths per minute or held-out campaign completion, our own measurements will be the only evidence for whether C1-C3 and R1-R4 work. Log them in a form others could compare against.
